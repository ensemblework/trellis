# Vision

Coding agents are extremely good at *doing*. Trellis is about *learning* —
an IDE + terminal where an AI agent watches what you're working on and
helps you build the skill yourself, instead of producing the answer for
you.

The shortest version: **autopilot vs. flight instructor.** Most AI coding
tools are an autopilot — you say where you want to go, it flies the plane.
Trellis is the instructor sitting in the other seat: hands off the
controls, but watching closely, ready to explain, correct, and point you
at the right resource, at your pace.

## Who this is for

Developers (junior through experienced) who want to actually learn a tool,
workflow, or concept — git internals, a new language, debugging a
specific class of error, reading a stack trace, writing their first
regex — rather than just get the task done once and forget how.

## What "success" looks like

A user opens Trellis, says "I want to learn how to resolve a merge
conflict," and by the end of the session they've done it themselves, with
the agent's guidance, and could do it again unaided next time — not
because they read an explanation, but because they *did it*, with
just-in-time nudges instead of a handed-over solution.

## Guardrails this vision implies

- The agent explains and points, it does not execute on your behalf by
  default.
- The agent respects your pace — it waits, it doesn't rush you to the
  answer, and it doesn't dump a wall of text when a single pointed hint
  will do.
- Cost and attention are both budgets: this should feel lightweight to use
  (fast, cheap, uncluttered), not like a second IDE's worth of chrome
  wrapped around a chatbot.
